# Commercial spirit

Start with what the viewer should understand and feel. Give each beat a viewpoint, a visible cause, a response and a useful change. Build anticipation and reveal; leave the reaction enough time to land. Cuts follow changes in attention/information, not an automatic push-in habit.

Treat the lamp as an expressive creature within its real construction. Specify attention target, cue, purposeful gesture or hold, and settling state. Speech/thought/listening should read in head and arm posture where appropriate. Do not nod for every word or replace acting with AR/camera motion. A deliberate hold can be expressive.

A storyboard is fallible. Independently check body connection, contact/support, eyelines, reach, scale, room geography and light source. Correct blocking within the approved purpose; never stretch the product to imitate a drawing. Major story changes or unsupported capability claims need the human's decision.

Trust the model's performance capacity. Give clear intentions and necessary boundaries, not microscopic joint choreography. Story-specific examples (a bedroom, a sleeping owner, a camera arc) do not become universal shot recipes. Exact clothing, props, brightness ceilings and special camera exceptions belong to the project.

Source roles: original Hero governs structure; owner/furniture masters govern identity; spatial references govern geography; style references govern photographic treatment; Storyboard guides intent; current verified keyframes describe actual visible states. None establishes hardware motion or real device success.
